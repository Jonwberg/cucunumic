## 0 · How to read this document

This appendix sits behind the investor deck. The deck tells the story; this document shows the work. Every number that appears in the deck is built from assumptions documented here. Where an assumption is anchored to an external source — a comparable transaction, a published market figure, a contractor rate — the source is cited. Where an assumption is internal — a modeled mix, a margin target, a ramp curve — it is flagged as such.

Two principles govern this document:

1. **Conservative is preferred over aggressive.** The base case is built on assumptions that an experienced hospitality LP can underwrite without flinching. The upside case demonstrates what the model can do; it does not anchor the ask.
2. **The math must reconcile.** Capex builds bottom-up to the all-in number. Revenue builds from occupancy × ADR × attach to the top-line. OpEx percentages tie to industry benchmarks. EBITDA margin sits at 32% — within the range mature boutique-luxury wellness destinations achieve at stabilization.

The financial story has four parts: **what it costs to build** (§2–§3), **what it earns** (§4–§5), **what it returns** (§6–§7), and **what could go wrong** (§9).

## 1 · Thesis (one paragraph)

Cucunumic is a Pacific-coast wellness destination opened complete on 11 hectares of escriturado private land in Baja California Sur. A 30-key boutique with the full integrated wellness program — six clinical-grade circuit cells, Spa Reception, Treatment House, Recovery Garden, F&B pavilion, pool, full back-of-house — opens in 2028. Phase 2 (expansion to 40 keys base or 50 keys upside) unlocks only when guest demand pulls beyond 30: trailing-12 ADR ≥ $510, occupancy ≥ 65% × 4 consecutive quarters, documented turnaway demand ≥ 15% × 2 consecutive peak seasons, modeled DSCR ≥ 1.5× on expansion debt. Phase 2 is funded by a separate raise at uplifted valuation. The integrated boutique is itself the validation of the brand and the price.

## 2 · Phase 1 Capital Stack — Where the $26M Goes

Phase 1 capital is built from the ground up at a construction rate of $50,000–$75,000 MXN per square meter of built area, which translates to roughly $2,630–$3,950 USD/m² at recent MXN/USD rates. This is the rate provided by the project owner based on local BCS contractor sounding and aligns with the lower end of premium boutique hospitality build cost in the region.

For reference, **Paradero Todos Santos** — a 35-key luxury boutique opened in 2021 in the same regional corridor (Todos Santos / Pescadero) — reportedly cost approximately $25M in 2021 dollars, which works out to roughly $714K per key all-in. Construction-cost inflation in BCS since 2021 has been on the order of 1.5×–2× for labor and materials. The Cucunumic build, anchored at the user-provided BCS rate, lands at approximately $866K per key all-in — directly comparable to Paradero's 2021 number with modest inflation accounted for.

The capital breaks into eleven buckets, each described below.

## 4 · Phase 1 Revenue Model — Where the $9.5M Comes From

Phase 1 stabilized revenue at Year 5 is **$9.5M**, broken into room, wellness, F&B, retail, and day-spa lines. The build is from the bottom up: occupancy × ADR yields room revenue; wellness attach yields the wellness line; F&B is benchmarked per occupied room-night.

### 4.1 Occupancy

Phase 1 stabilized occupancy is modeled at **65%**, which lands below the Los Cabos market average (75% per FITURCA April 2025 data) and below mature destination boutique averages. Two reasons for the conservative position:

1. **New brand, no operating history.** The first three years are a brand-building period where occupancy ramps from 30% (Y1, half-year) toward stabilized levels. Booking it at the market average from day one would imply we can match Paradero / Esperanza occupancy without their decade of brand equity.
2. **Wellness destination guests stay longer.** Average length of stay (LOS) for a wellness-program guest is 3.5–5 nights (vs. 2.5 nights for transient luxury), which improves operating economics but constrains turnover. The 65% occupancy assumption reflects a deliberate mix of longer-stay package guests.

The seasonal mix that yields the 65% blended rate:
- Peak (Nov–Apr, six months): 80% occupancy
- Shoulder (May, October, ~two months): 60% occupancy
- Off-peak (June–September, four months): 45% occupancy
- Weighted average: **~65%**

### 4.2 Average Daily Rate (ADR)

Phase 1 stabilized ADR is modeled at **$510**, positioned defensibly within the Los Cabos comp set.

| Comp | Stabilized ADR (USD) | Source / dataset |
|---|---|---|
| Los Cabos market average | $473 | FITURCA, April 2025 |
| Los Cabos scenic-route segment | $802 | FITURCA, February 2025 |
| Paradero Todos Santos (boutique wellness) | $700–800 | Published rates, 2024–2025 |
| Esperanza Auberge | $850–1,200 | Published rates, 2024–2025 |
| Habitas Tulum (comparable brand, different geography) | ~$650 | Published rates, 2024 |
| **Cucunumic target** | **$510** | Modeled — between market average and Paradero |

The Cucunumic ADR sits at +$37 above the Los Cabos market average and -$190 below Paradero. The rationale: Cucunumic does not yet have Paradero's brand recognition, but its property quality (11 ha private estate, custom architecture, full clinical wellness program) substantially exceeds the average Cabo hotel. The +$37 premium over market is supportable; the discount to Paradero leaves headroom to grow into Paradero pricing as the brand matures.

Seasonal ADR variation tracks the seasonal occupancy variation:
- Peak: $650–750 ADR
- Shoulder: $450–500 ADR
- Off-peak: $380–420 ADR
- Weighted average: **~$510**

Wellness is the primary revenue lever beyond rooms. The model assumes **60% of guests purchase a wellness package** at stabilization, with the remaining 30% buying à la carte services and 10% staying room-only.

Wellness packages are the primary product. Three tiers:

| Package | Length | Per person | Y5 buyer mix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wellness Sampler | 3 nights | $1,200 | 20% |
| **Reset Program** | 5 nights | $2,500 | 60% |
| Intensive Recovery | 7 nights | $4,500 | 20% |

The **Reset Program** is the anchor — 5 nights at $500 per person per night on top of room rate. Two guests on a Reset Program contribute $1,000/night in wellness revenue on top of the $510 room rate, more than doubling per-room-night economics versus a room-only stay.

The blended wellness revenue per occupied room-night at Y5 stabilization (60% package penetration, 30% à la carte at ~$100 per person per night, 10% room-only):
- Package guests: 2 × 60% × $450/night blended package average = **$540 per ORN**
- À la carte guests: 2 × 30% × $100/night blended = **$60 per ORN**
- Room-only: 2 × 10% × $0 = **$0 per ORN**

The package penetration follows a ramp:
- Y1: 40% (new brand, lower trust)
- Y2: 45%
- Y3: 50%
- Y4: 55%
- Y5: 60% (stabilized)

This penetration ramp is the single most leveraged assumption in the revenue model. Each 5-percentage-point change in stabilized penetration moves Y5 EBITDA by approximately $110K.

### 5.2 Management structure (TBD at raise structuring)

The G&A line includes a 4% blended management fee, which assumes either an independent management company (typically 3–5% of revenue) or a branded operator (typically 6–10% but inclusive of brand and distribution value). The decision between independent and branded is deferred to raise structuring and discussion with prospective LPs.

- **Independent management company** (e.g., Indagare-aligned boutique operator) — 3–5% of revenue + 15% incentive over hurdle. Preserves sponsor brand control while bringing in disciplined hospitality operating capability. Roughly $400K/year in fees against $9.5M revenue.
- **Branded operator** (Habitas, Auberge, Capella, Six Senses) — 6–10% of revenue + brand fee + 20% incentive. Brings distribution and brand on top of operations, but the sponsor cedes brand control. Roughly $700K–$1M/year in fees.

The 4% modeling assumption sits between these two — a midpoint that covers either choice.

### 6.1 The ramp explained

**Year 1 (2028)** is half a year of operations following an H1 opening. The property opens with 32 FTE on payroll (most hired 60–90 days before opening for training) and aggressive launch marketing. Revenue is roughly 25% of stabilized, but operating costs are nearly at full run-rate. The Y1 EBITDA loss of $1.84M is funded from the $1.0M working capital reserve baked into the Phase 1 raise plus the contingency allocation. Investors should expect Y1 to be a cash burn year — this is normal for boutique luxury openings.

**Year 2 (2029)** is the first full year of operations. Revenue is at 50% of stabilized capacity as the brand begins to build organic word-of-mouth and the wellness program demonstrates outcomes. EBITDA loss narrows to $370K, comfortably absorbed by the remaining working capital reserve.

**Year 3 (2030)** is the inflection. Revenue clears 75% of stabilized, EBITDA crosses zero and reaches $1.12M (18% margin). Cumulative operating loss from Y1+Y2 has been absorbed and the property begins generating free cash flow.

**Year 4 (2031)** is near-stabilized. Revenue at 90%, EBITDA at $2.15M (27% margin), cumulative cash flow turns positive. This is also when the Phase 2 gate measurement period concludes (Apr 2031 gate clearance is base case).

**Year 5 (2032)** is the first stabilized year. Revenue at $9.5M, EBITDA at $3.05M (32% margin), free cash flow at $2.78M. This is the cash flow basis on which Phase 2 valuation is calculated.

### 6.2 Path-to-profitability milestones

| Milestone | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Operating EBITDA breakeven | Y3 (2030) | Revenue covers all operating costs. Project funds itself month-over-month. |
| Free cash flow positive | Y3 (2030) | After maintenance capex reserve, project generates positive cash. |
| Cumulative EBITDA breakeven | Y4 (2031) | Y1+Y2 opening losses recovered from later operating profit. |
| Project debt-serviceable | Y3 (2030) | EBITDA ≥ $1.0M supports ~$7M of construction debt at 1.5× DSCR (relevant if any Phase 1 debt). |
| Phase 2 gate measurement starts | Y3 (2030) | First 4-quarter ADR/occupancy window opens; turnaway tracking starts at opening. |
| **Phase 2 gate plausibly clears** | **Y4 Q2 (Apr 2031)** | Gate criteria sustained; Phase 2 raise begins. |
| Investor cash-on-cash payback | Y6–Y7 | Cumulative LP distributions + Phase 2 uplift recover initial $26M equity. |
| Investor IRR realization | Y5–Y8 (terminal) | Exit at 10–13× stabilized EBITDA, or refinance + hold. |

## 7 · Returns & Valuation

### 7.1 Phase 1 standalone returns

| Metric | Conservative | Base case | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Y5 occupancy | 55% | 65% | 72% |
| Y5 ADR | $475 | $510 | $580 |
| Y5 package penetration | 50% | 60% | 75% |
| Y5 Revenue | $7.4M | $9.52M | $11.8M |
| **Y5 EBITDA** | **$1.6M** | **$3.05M** | **$4.0M** |
| **Y5 EBITDA margin** | 22% | 32% | 34% |
| **5-year IRR** | **10–13%** | **15–19%** | **21–25%** |
| **Equity multiple (5-yr terminal)** | **1.6–1.9×** | **2.6–3.0×** | **3.4–4.0×** |

The **base case 15–19% IRR** is the modeled investment return for an LP putting equity in at Phase 1 closing and exiting at Y5 via either property sale or refinance-and-hold. The IRR is calculated assuming:

- Capital deployed Y0 (2027)
- Distributions Y2–Y5 as cash becomes available (modest in Y2, ~$0.4M; rising to ~$2.0M by Y5)
- Terminal cash event at Y5 — either sale at 11× stabilized EBITDA ($33.6M asset value, after-tax distribution to LPs) or refinance with debt against the stabilized EBITDA returning roughly equivalent cash

The IRR is sensitive to the terminal multiple (each 1× of multiple moves IRR by ~3 percentage points) and to the Phase 2 outcome (Phase 2 layered on raises combined IRR; Phase 2 not triggered keeps Phase 1 IRR but reduces total exit value).

### 7.2 Y5 hotel valuation — three scenarios, two methods

**Method 1 — EBITDA multiple (going-concern valuation):**

| Scenario | Y5 EBITDA | Multiple | Rationale | **Y5 Valuation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | $1.6M | 8× | Boutique without proven brand; thin BCS comp transactions | **$12.8M** |
| Base case | $3.05M | 11× | Boutique luxury with wellness anchor; mid of 10–14× range | **$33.6M** |
| Optimistic | $4.0M | 13× | Aman / Habitas / Lanserhof precedent; premium wellness destination | **$52.0M** |

**Method 2 — Per-key transaction comps (cross-check):**

| Scenario | $/key | × 30 keys | **Y5 Valuation** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | $430K | | $12.9M |
| Base case | $1.12M | | $33.6M |
| Optimistic | $1.73M | | $52M |

The two methods triangulate within ~$1M across all three scenarios. This convergence is a quality check on the underlying assumptions — independent valuation approaches arriving at the same answer suggests the model is internally consistent.

**Asset floor (downside protection):**

If Y5 going-concern value falls below deployed capital (the conservative scenario), the asset basis floors valuation:
- Capital deployed: $26M
- Less depreciation (~10% over 5 years): $23.4M
- Plus land appreciation (~8% annual × 5 years on $3M land basis): +$1.4M
- **Asset floor: ~$24.8M**

Even in the conservative case, LPs are protected by the underlying asset basis. The Phase 1 LPs may not earn their target IRR, but the equity-floor risk is materially reduced by the embedded asset value in the property and land.

## 8 · Phase 2 Capital Raise — Time-to-Close

In the success case, the time from Phase 1 raise (2027) to Phase 2 capital available is approximately **4 years**, broken into three phases.

### 8.1 Gate clearance (the binding event)

The gate isn't cleared the day the metrics first turn green — each criterion has a measurement window. The slowest-binding window determines the date.

| Criterion | Earliest possible clearance | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Trailing-12 ADR ≥ $510 | Y3 Q3 (mid-2030) | Need 12 months above $510 |
| Occupancy ≥ 65% × 4 consecutive Q | Y3 Q4 (end 2030) | First quarter likely Y3 Q1; 4 consecutive Q = Y3 Q1 → Y3 Q4 |
| **Turnaway ≥ 15% × 2 consecutive peak seasons** | **Y4 Q2 (Apr 2031)** | **Binding constraint.** Need 2 peak seasons (Nov–Apr) |
| DSCR ≥ 1.5× modeled | Y3 Q4 | Not binding in success scenarios |

**Earliest realistic gate clearance: April 2031** — three years after Phase 1 opens.

### 8.2 Capital raise process

Smart sponsor strategy is to pre-market during the gate measurement period so that capital is committed before the gate formally clears.

| Phase | Months from gate clearance |
|---|---|
| Pre-marketing during Y3 H2 | -6 to 0 |
| Formal launch (gate-clearance day) | 0 |
| Initial diligence | 0 to 2 |
| Term sheet | 2 to 3 |
| Documentation | 3 to 5 |
| First close | 5 to 6 |
| Final close | 6 to 9 |

**Standard total: 6–9 months from gate clearance to Phase 2 capital deployable.**

### 8.3 Compressing the timeline — accelerators

Four mechanisms can compress the time from gate clearance to first close from 6–9 months down to approximately 60 days. Each has a modest cost and a meaningful time benefit:

| Accelerator | Time saved | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 LPs sign pro-rata rights at Phase 1 closing | 1–2 months | Legal complexity in Phase 1 docs |
| Pre-engage debt provider during Y3 | 2–3 months | Some fees during diligence period |
| Phase 2 footprint pre-permitted with Phase 1 | 4–6 months | $50–100K extra A&E + permit work in Y0 |
| Audited Y2 + Y3 financials by Big-4 or equivalent | 1–2 months | $40–80K annual audit cost |

With all four accelerators, **first close lands within 60 days of gate clearance**.

### 8.4 Variance under different ramp speeds

| Ramp scenario | Gate clears | Phase 2 close | Phase 2 opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aggressive (12 months ahead) | Apr 2030 | Aug 2030 (3 mo with accelerators) | early 2032 (Y5 H1) |
| **Base case** | **Apr 2031 (Y4 Q2)** | **Jul 2031 (3 mo with accelerators)** | **early 2033 (Y6 H1)** |
| Conservative (12 months behind) | Apr 2032 (Y5 Q2) | Jan 2033 (9 mo without accelerators) | early 2035 (Y8 H1) |

## 9 · Key Risks & Mitigants

Every material risk to the model is documented below with its mitigation strategy.

### 9.1 Construction cost overrun

The single largest capital risk. The $50–75k MXN/m² rate is project-owner local-knowledge level; a real bid could come in higher. Sensitivity: a 15% capex overrun would push Phase 1 from $26M to $29.9M, dropping base-case IRR from 15–19% to roughly 12–15%.

**Mitigants:**
- Obtain bid-quality construction estimate from at least two Cabo / La Paz contractors before raise closes. Use the higher bid for the modeled budget.
- 10% contingency built into the $26M reflects an industry norm but not aggressive overrun protection. Investors should expect contingency to be substantially drawn.
- Phased construction sequencing (foundations and shell before high-spec finish work) allows for spec adjustment mid-build if costs trend high.

### 9.2 Demand uncertainty during ramp

A new wellness destination in BCS with no operating history must build a customer base from zero. The 65% stabilized occupancy assumption is conservative against the 75% Cabo market average but still requires the property to find its audience.

**Mitigants:**
- Heavy pre-opening marketing ($500K in Y0 + $760K in Y1) seeds the brand.
- Wellness retreat groups (corporate, women's, athlete recovery) provide bulk bookings that smooth occupancy during ramp.
- The Reset Program (5 nights × $2,500 per person) is structurally less price-sensitive than transient luxury — guests who buy a wellness program have already pre-committed.
- If demand under-performs through Y3, the model's downside is the conservative case (10–13% IRR), still positive returns, not a write-down.

### 9.3 Off-grid operational reliability

The property's brand promise includes off-grid sustainability. If PV/battery/water systems fail to deliver expected uptime, guest experience degrades and operating costs (diesel backup, water trucking) spike.

**Mitigants:**
- 300 kWh battery storage provides ~3 days of operation without solar generation. Backup generator (60 kW diesel) covers extended cloudy periods.
- Wastewater MBR is a proven technology in remote hospitality (used by Esperanza, multiple Aman properties); not experimental.
- Engineering / maintenance lead (2 FTE) is hired pre-opening with specific solar/water/wastewater certification.
- Off-grid uptime becomes a published KPI for the operating model, tracked quarterly.

### 9.4 Wellness program credibility

The clinical-grade wellness offering (HBOT, IV therapy, integrative consultations) requires medical/clinical credentialing to operate legally and credibly in Mexico. The wellness director role is a key dependency.

**Mitigants:**
- Wellness director recruited 12+ months before opening with explicit clinical credentials (MD or equivalent recognized in Mexico).
- Partnerships with US/Mexican wellness practitioners for visiting-fellow programs in the first 2 years to build initial credibility.
- HBOT operator credentialing (UHMS / NBDHMT) is a standardized international qualification; recruiting from US or European pool is feasible.

### 9.5 Mexican regulatory / political risk

BCS hospitality projects are exposed to Mexican federal and state regulation: FIDETUR permits, environmental impact studies, tourism zoning, foreign-ownership restrictions on certain coastal property types. Cucunumic is escriturado (title cleared), which removes the most acute legal risk, but ongoing regulatory exposure persists.

**Mitigants:**
- Pre-development phase ($0.25M budget within the $26M) includes specialist Mexican land-use counsel.
- Project structure can be configured to comply with foreign-ownership rules via fideicomiso (bank trust) if any of the Phase 1 LPs are non-Mexican.
- Sponsor (project owner) is Mexican-resident, which simplifies several compliance dimensions.

### 9.6 Phase 2 gate not clearing

The optionality of Phase 2 is a feature of the model, but it also means Phase 1 LPs may not see the combined returns. If gate doesn't clear, Phase 1 stabilized economics are what they are — 15–19% IRR at base case, with the asset basis as downside floor.

**Mitigants:**
- Phase 1 is designed to be a complete operating business on its own. The model does not depend on Phase 2 for Phase 1 LPs to earn their returns.
- The four-condition gate is designed to be achievable in a success scenario but not gameable. If the gate doesn't clear, that's a signal that Phase 2 expansion would have been over-built, and the discipline of waiting prevents bad capital allocation.

## 11 · What an LP Should Ask Next

The most leveraged diligence items, ranked by impact on the model.

1. **Validated construction cost.** Get at least two bid-quality estimates from BCS contractors against the locked Phase 1 scope. This single piece of information could move IRR by ±3pp.
2. **Wellness director shortlist.** Pre-identification of the clinical lead is the highest-stakes hire and the longest-lead-time recruit. Concrete shortlist of named candidates with clinical credentials and Mexican-market familiarity reduces ramp risk.
3. **Pre-bookings or LOIs from wellness retreat partners.** Group bookings during the first 6 months of Year 1 would materially de-risk the ramp curve and validate the $510 ADR positioning.
4. **Phase 2 footprint permitting strategy.** Whether to pre-permit Phase 2 with Phase 1 (adds $50–100K to Phase 0, accelerates Phase 2 close by 4–6 months) is a structural decision.
5. **Management structure** — independent operator vs. branded operator. Affects the G&A line ($400K vs. $1M range), brand control, and distribution. Should be resolved before Phase 1 raise close.
