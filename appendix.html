Thesis

One paragraph.

Cucunumic is a Pacific-coast wellness destination opened complete on 11 hectares of escriturado private land in Baja California Sur. A 30-key boutique with the full integrated wellness program — six clinical-grade circuit cells, Spa Reception, Treatment House, Recovery Garden, F&B pavilion, pool, full back-of-house — opens in 2028. Phase 2 (expansion to 40 keys base or 50 keys upside) unlocks only when guest demand pulls beyond 30: trailing-12 ADR ≥ $510, occupancy ≥ 65% × 4 consecutive quarters, documented turnaway demand ≥ 15% × 2 consecutive peak seasons, modeled DSCR ≥ 1.5× on expansion debt. Phase 2 is funded by a separate raise at uplifted valuation. The integrated boutique is itself the validation of the brand and the price.

Phase 1 Capital Stack

Where the $22.7M goes, bucket by bucket.

Phase 1 capital is built from the ground up at a construction rate of $40,000–$60,000 MXN per square meter of built area — roughly $2,100–$3,160 USD/m² at recent exchange rates. This is the rate provided by the project owner from local BCS contractor sounding, aligned with the lower end of premium boutique hospitality build cost in the region.

Comp anchor
Paradero Todos Santos — a 35-key luxury boutique opened in 2021 in the same regional corridor — reportedly cost approximately $25M in 2021 dollars, or ~$714K per key all-in. With BCS construction inflation since 2021 in the 1.5–2× range, Cucunumic at $757K per key sits directly comparable.

The casita rate sits at the top of the construction-cost band because the architectural concept calls for genuine custom materials (rammed earth load-bearing walls, lime plaster, locally milled timber, deep climate-responsive eaves). The wellness shells take a middle rate — substantial mechanical and electrical fit-out, less custom material expression. Reception and F&B take a slightly lower rate because they're semi-public and design-driven but use more conventional construction.

What would move this

  • Validated contractor estimate against locked scope — the single highest-leverage diligence step. Could move the number ±15% in either direction.

  • Material spec choices (rammed earth vs. concrete + plaster; imported vs. domestic stone; bespoke vs. catalog FFE) can move the keys bucket alone by ±$2M.

Phase 2 Capital — Earned Expansion

Two scenarios. Both gated.

Phase 2 builds out the property to 40 keys base (10 added) or 50 keys upside (20 added — 10 suites + 10 villas). The premium positioning of these added keys earns a higher per-key build budget because the units are larger, more privately positioned, and require their own service approach.

The deck cites a Phase 2 raise size of $18–22M. This range includes the construction capex above plus working capital reserve and a buffer for any construction debt origination costs. It also allows the Phase 2 raise to fund some maintenance capex on the Phase 1 building stock that would have accumulated by 2031–2033.

Uplift mechanic
Phase 2 raises at uplifted valuation reflecting Phase 1 stabilized cash flow. If Phase 1 hits $3.0M+ EBITDA at gate clearance, the implied asset value at 11× is $33M — uplifting the basis for new Phase 2 equity to roughly 1.45× the $22.7M Phase 1 raise. This is why Phase 1 LPs benefit from holding through Phase 2 even if they don't participate in the second raise.

Phase 1 Revenue Model

Where the $9.5M comes from, per occupied room-night.

Phase 1 stabilized revenue at Year 5 is $9.5M, broken into room, wellness, F&B, retail, and day-spa lines. The build is from the bottom up: occupancy × ADR yields room revenue; wellness attach yields the wellness line; F&B is benchmarked per occupied room-night.

4.1 · Occupancy — modeled at 65% at stabilization

Below the Los Cabos market average (75% per FITURCA April 2025) and below mature destination boutique averages. Two reasons for the conservative position: first, no operating history — the brand ramps from 30% (Y1 half-year) toward stabilized levels; second, wellness destination guests stay 3.5–5 nights (vs. 2.5 for transient luxury), improving operating economics but constraining turnover.

Seasonal mix yielding the 65% blended rate: peak (Nov–Apr, 6 mo) at 80% · shoulder (May, October) at 60% · off-peak (Jun–Sep, 4 mo) at 45%.

5.2 · Management Structure — TBD at Raise Structuring

Independent management company (e.g., Indagare-aligned boutique operator) — 3–5% of revenue + 15% incentive. Preserves sponsor brand control. ~$400K/year against $9.5M revenue.

Branded operator (Habitas, Auberge, Capella, Six Senses) — 6–10% of revenue + brand fee + 20% incentive. Brings distribution and brand. ~$700K–$1M/year.

The 4% modeling assumption sits between these — a midpoint that covers either choice.

Key Risks & Mitigants

Six risks, each with its mitigation strategy.

9.1 · Construction Cost Overrun

The single largest capital risk. The $40–60k MXN/m² rate is project-owner local-knowledge level; a real bid could come in higher. Sensitivity: a 15% capex overrun pushes Phase 1 to $26.1M, dropping base-case IRR to roughly 15–18%.

Mitigants: obtain bid-quality estimates from at least two Cabo / La Paz contractors before raise close. 10% contingency built into $22.7M. Phased construction sequencing allows mid-build spec adjustment.

9.2 · Demand Uncertainty During Ramp

A new wellness destination in BCS with no operating history must build a customer base from zero. The 65% stabilized occupancy is conservative against 75% Cabo market average but still requires the property to find its audience.

Mitigants: heavy pre-opening marketing ($500K Y0 + $760K Y1). Wellness retreat groups (corporate, women's, athlete recovery) provide bulk bookings. Reset Program is structurally less price-sensitive. Downside is the conservative case (12–15% IRR), still positive, not a write-down.

9.3 · Off-Grid Operational Reliability

The brand promise includes off-grid sustainability. If PV/battery/water fails to deliver expected uptime, guest experience degrades and operating costs spike.

Mitigants: 300 kWh battery provides ~3 days of operation without solar. 60 kW diesel backup. MBR wastewater is proven in remote hospitality (Esperanza, Aman precedents). Maintenance lead with solar/water certification hired pre-opening.

9.4 · Wellness Program Credibility

Clinical-grade wellness offering requires medical credentialing to operate legally and credibly in Mexico. The wellness director is a key dependency.

Mitigants: wellness director recruited 12+ months before opening with explicit clinical credentials. Visiting-fellow programs in Years 1–2 build credibility. HBOT operator credentialing (UHMS / NBDHMT) is internationally standardized.

9.5 · Mexican Regulatory · Political Risk

BCS hospitality projects are exposed to federal and state regulation: FIDETUR permits, environmental impact studies, foreign-ownership restrictions. Cucunumic is escriturado, which removes the most acute legal risk.

Mitigants: pre-development phase includes specialist Mexican land-use counsel. Structure compatible with fideicomiso for non-Mexican LPs. Sponsor is Mexican-resident.

9.6 · Phase 2 Gate Not Clearing

Phase 2 optionality is a feature, but it also means Phase 1 LPs may not see combined returns. If gate doesn't clear, Phase 1 standalone economics deliver — 18–22% IRR base, with asset basis as downside floor.

Mitigants: Phase 1 is designed to be a complete operating business standalone. The gate is designed to be achievable in success scenarios but not gameable. If the gate doesn't clear, that's a signal Phase 2 expansion would have been over-built — the discipline prevents bad capital allocation.

What an LP Should Ask Next

Five leveraged diligence items, ranked by impact.

  1. Validated construction cost. Get at least two bid-quality estimates from BCS contractors against the locked Phase 1 scope. This single piece of information could move IRR by ±3pp.
  2. Wellness director shortlist. Pre-identification of the clinical lead is the highest-stakes hire and the longest-lead-time recruit. Named candidates with clinical credentials and Mexican-market familiarity de-risk the ramp.
  3. Pre-bookings or LOIs from wellness retreat partners. Group bookings during the first 6 months of Year 1 would materially de-risk the ramp curve and validate the $510 ADR positioning.
  4. Phase 2 footprint permitting strategy. Whether to pre-permit Phase 2 with Phase 1 (adds $50–100K to Phase 0, accelerates Phase 2 close by 4–6 months) is a structural decision.
  5. Management structure — independent operator vs. branded operator. Affects the G&A line ($400K vs. $1M range), brand control, and distribution. Should be resolved before Phase 1 raise close.
