01

Three tiers at a glance

The same wellness brand at three capital sizes — different theses, different investor fit.

Cucunumic's wellness destination thesis works at scale. The economics improve as the property grows because clinical-grade wellness has fixed costs — equipment, certifications, specialist staff — that get absorbed more efficiently over more keys. That same scale logic means smaller tiers operate under different brand promises: a 10-key Wellness Retreat trades clinical depth for intimate exclusivity; a 20-key Wellness Boutique hits a middle ground; the 30-key Wellness Destination is the full-program flagship.

Each tier is a complete, independently investable business. The choice is about investor appetite — capital size, return profile, and brand-versus-yield orientation.

The scaling logic
Per-key capex improves as the property grows ($1.10M → $945K → $866K). The wellness program's fixed costs spread over more keys; site work, off-grid plant, and soft costs benefit from economies of scale. EBITDA margin similarly scales: 22% at 10 keys, 28% at 20, 32% at 30. The 30-key destination isn't arbitrary — it's where the model crosses into venture-tier returns.

The Wellness Retreat is the smallest expression of the brand: ten luxury casitas on the same 11-hectare estate, a focused three-modality health center (sauna + cold plunge, hydrotherapy, treatment rooms — no HBOT, no IV clinic), and an intimate F&B program. Less destination, more residence.

The return profile is built around land appreciation and brand option value as much as operating cash flow. At this scale, the wellness program is an amenity rather than a clinical anchor; the ADR premium comes from privacy, view, and design. For patient capital that wants exposure to the Pacific-coast BCS land thesis with modest yield and a future option to participate in the larger 30-key build, this is the cleanest entry.

2.4 · Returns and exit

The Tier 1 exit case is dominated by asset basis + land appreciation, not operating multiple. Y5 going-concern at 9× EBITDA = $4.5M, well below capital deployed. The investor is protected by the underlying $11M asset basis appreciating at ~7% annually (mix of land + buildings).

Five-year terminal: ~$13–14M total returns (asset basis + 5 years of operating distributions of $1.0–1.2M cumulative). IRR lands at 7–11%, modest hospitality-LP / patient-capital territory. The accelerator is brand option value: if the project scales toward Tier 2 or Tier 3 down the line, Tier 1 LPs hold first-look rights at the expansion entry valuation.

Best-fit investor

  • Family office wanting Pacific-coast BCS land exposure with modest cash yield
  • Patient capital willing to underwrite asset basis as the primary return driver
  • Wellness-aligned LP using Tier 1 as a relationship anchor for potential Tier 2/3 participation
  • Individual investors at the sub-$2M check size who can pool to fill the round

3.4 · Returns and exit

At 20 keys, the model crosses the line into standard hospitality LP returns. Y5 going-concern valuation at 10× EBITDA = $16.5M, against $19M deployed. Asset basis floor: ~$22M (capital deployed + land appreciation − depreciation). Investors are partially protected by basis, with operating returns supplying the rest.

Five-year terminal: ~$22–24M (going-concern multiple expanding toward asset basis + $2.5M cumulative distributions Y2–Y5). IRR 12–16%, equity multiple 2.2–2.6×. This is the tier where boutique-luxury hospitality LPs and family offices intersect comfortably.

Best-fit investor

  • Mid-market boutique hospitality LP (typical check size $2–5M)
  • Wellness-aligned family office wanting clinical anchor + brand exposure
  • Real-estate-oriented capital with hospitality experience
  • Investors who want clinical wellness exposure but at lower capital commitment than Tier 3

05

Choosing a tier

Match the tier to the appetite, not the other way around.

The three tiers are not stages of the same project — they are distinct investments that share a brand, a site, and an operating philosophy. Each tier is fundable on its own. The choice depends on three orthogonal questions:

1 · Capital size

Check size sets the floor. Tier 1 can be filled by $0.5–2M individual checks pooled into an ~$11M round. Tier 2 expects $2–5M institutional or family-office checks. Tier 3 typically wants $3–10M anchor checks and a few smaller co-investors.

2 · Return profile

Tier 1 returns are asset-backed and patient — 7–11% IRR with strong asset basis floor, suited to capital that values land appreciation and brand option value as much as cash yield. Tier 2 returns are standard hospitality — 12–16% IRR, mid-market boutique-luxury territory. Tier 3 returns are wellness-destination scale — 15–19% Phase 1 IRR, with combined Y10 IRR expanding to 17–22% if Phase 2 unlocks.

3 · Brand thesis alignment

Tier 1 is private and intimate; the wellness program is an amenity, not a destination identity. Tier 2 starts to build a recognizable wellness anchor (HBOT, sound+breath) but stops short of clinical positioning. Tier 3 commits to the wellness destination thesis fully — clinical-grade, IV-clinic capable, full-program. Investors aligned with the “wellness destination, opened complete” brand promise belong in Tier 3.

Hybrid pathways
These tiers can be combined or sequenced. A natural hybrid: start at Tier 1 or Tier 2, with explicit option rights to participate in a Tier 3 expansion raise within 5 years. The sponsor preserves the brand thesis at Tier 3 while the LP base scales over multiple raises. Worth discussing case-by-case during raise structuring.

Common ground across all tiers

  • Construction rate: $50–75k MXN/m² · same BCS contractor-sounding rate across all three
  • Land contribution: 11 hectares · same escriturado private estate · negotiated value
  • Off-grid commitment: all tiers fully off-grid (sized to scale)
  • Brand: “Cucunumic · Baja Wellness” identity is consistent
  • Management: independent operator (3–5% fee) or branded operator (6–10%) — TBD at raise structuring
  • Asset basis floor: all three protected by the underlying real estate
